## What Worked

- **Fresh live adjudication beat the literal s01 inventory.** s01's coverage-report.md was three live-run
  slices stale by the time s10 fired; re-scanning `docs --coverage` immediately before the dry-run gave
  an accurate, self-consistent adjudication baseline (and caught the 2 new slice09/10 plan nodes, the 4
  unmigrated ODDs, and the true artifact-family count) that a literal diff against the old report would
  have missed or misreported.
- **Discovering the drift-skip need, the date-derivation gap, the decomposition-drift interaction, and the
  containment display gap all happened *during* dry-run/verify, before the irreversible commit** — each
  was fixed, tested, and the whole sequence re-run from a clean discard, rather than committing first and
  patching after. The one-clean-commit discipline held even though the path to it took five separate
  code fixes.
- **The operator's direct review of the live `node list` output caught two real gaps** (the date question,
  the artifact-orphaning display question) that no amount of fixture testing would have surfaced — a
  concrete case for looking at the actual rendered result on a real, large corpus before calling a slice
  done, not just trusting green tests.
- **A scripted, whole-corpus structural cross-check** (250/251 files, not a hand-picked sample) caught and
  explained the one exception cleanly, building real confidence in the containment logic beyond what
  fixture tests over a handful of synthetic cases could.
